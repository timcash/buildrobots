# U.S. robotics policy and Utah manufacturing context

**Policy snapshot · November 5, 2026**

The Build Robots proposal focuses on U.S. production of five recurring robotics inputs: servomotors, precision gear reducers, EtherCAT servo drives, industrial fiber lasers, and machine-vision cameras. The proposed Utah campus would share production and test equipment across firms and use buyer-backed qualification lots to establish demand before adding capacity.

## Industry priorities and deployment rules

- **Association for Advancing Automation (A3).** Its national strategy advocates a federal robotics office and commission, adoption incentives, public-sector deployment, workforce training, research support, and updated standards. A3 publishes the R15.06 series for industrial robots and R15.08 for industrial mobile robots. Identify the standards applicable to each finished system during pilot design. [A3 strategy](https://www.automate.org/robotics/news/a3-releases-vision-for-a-u-s-national-robotics-strategy) · [A3 industrial robot standards](https://www.automate.org/store/categories/industrial-robot-standards)
- **Association for Uncrewed Vehicle Systems International (AUVSI).** Its Partnership for Robotics Competitiveness advocates a national strategy that includes domestic industrial capacity, secure supply chains, workforce development, and standards for emerging robotics. [AUVSI partnership launch and policy paper](https://www.auvsi.org/news/auvsi-launches-bipartisan-partnership-for-robotics-competitiveness-on-capitol-hill/)
- **Special Competitive Studies Project (SCSP).** The independent commission's preliminary report connects limited U.S. deployment with integration costs, skills gaps, unpredictable demand, and supplier constraints. A Utah pilot can measure those factors through actual orders, commissioning time, and qualification results. [SCSP preliminary report](https://www.scsp.ai/robotics-preliminary-report/)
- **State and local deployment.** San Mateo County adopted a registration and permit ordinance for covered commercial mobile autonomous robots in unincorporated areas. Texas began enforcing its authorization requirement on May 28, 2026, for covered commercial driverless motor vehicles carrying passengers or goods. These are examples of rules that apply to specific systems and operating locations. [San Mateo County ordinance record](https://sanmateocounty.legistar.com/LegislationDetail.aspx?GUID=8335563B-EFE7-487F-B47B-A593E334A831&ID=8236925) · [TxDMV enforcement notice](https://www.txdmv.gov/sites/default/files/body-files/2026-06-02_Automated_Vehicle_Enforcement_System.pdf)

## Utah campus and Foreign-Trade Zone 30

A **state-backed manufacturing campus** would organize tenants, shared equipment, utilities, workforce training, and buyer qualification. Massachusetts offers a precedent for shared equipment and test capacity. Its RoboBench program announced about $6.6 million in awards to four organizations in August 2026. Utah's Manufacturing Modernization Grant statute provides an existing framework for eligible Utah businesses and specified costs such as equipment, production engineering, and specialized training. A dedicated robotics track would be a policy proposal. [Massachusetts RoboBench awards](https://innovation.masstech.org/robobench) · [Utah Code § 63N-3-1102](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title63N/Chapter3/C63N-3-S1102_2026050620260506.pdf)

**Foreign-Trade Zone 30** is a separate federal customs mechanism. World Trade Center Utah is the grantee. Confirm the designation pathway for a specific parcel, U.S. Customs and Border Protection activation, and any FTZ Board authorization needed for the actual production activity and foreign inputs. Model eligible duty deferral for goods entering U.S. commerce and duty treatment for qualifying re-exports using actual import classifications and origins. Compare those benefits with operating and inventory-control costs. [FTZ 30 record](https://ofis.trade.gov/Zones/Details/21) · [Commerce FTZ overview](https://www.trade.gov/about-ftzs) · [Commerce production authorization guidance](https://www.trade.gov/ftz-production-page)

## Component qualification priorities

The following are proposed starting points. Use the 100-entry component appendix to identify the parts and processes behind each pilot and to preserve the proposed sourcing categories.

| Component family | Candidate Utah work | Evidence to collect on the first lot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Servomotor | Winding, impregnation, rotor balancing, housing machining, and feedback alignment | Torque and thermal performance, insulation test results, and encoder alignment |
| Precision gear reducer | Start with planetary gears and housings. Qualify specialist heat treatment and finishing with suppliers. | Tooth geometry, backlash, stiffness, and endurance |
| EtherCAT servo drive | Assemble boards and enclosures and integrate thermal management and feedback interfaces. | Current-sense accuracy, temperature rise, EtherCAT interoperability, and EMC screening |
| Industrial fiber laser | Integrate fiber, controls, cooling, and the processing head. Share enclosed test space. | Optical loss, beam quality, thermal stability, and interlock verification |
| Machine-vision camera | Assemble boards and housings and align the sensor, optics, and trigger inputs. | Image-quality metrics, trigger timing, calibration repeatability, and network interoperability |

Start with one motion package and one optics application. For motion, qualify the motor, reducer, and drive together against a buyer's duty cycle. For optics, qualify a camera for inspection or a fiber-laser module for welding against the buyer's process. Keep the separate test requirements for each component, plus system-level acceptance criteria for the integrated application.

## Evidence for a funding decision

1. **Demand.** Obtain a buyer's target price, volume range, acceptance tests, and a paid qualification commitment. Where a Utah public buyer uses an eligible trial-use contract, document the follow-on procurement path and any potential budget. The buyer retains discretion over a subsequent purchase. [Utah Code § 63G-6a-802.3](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/title63g/chapter6a/C63G-6a_1800010118000101.pdf)
2. **Supply.** Record the part revision, manufacturing origin, quoted lead time, primary and alternate supplier, and qualification status. Track Utah production activity and verified U.S.-origin inputs separately.
3. **Production.** Tie proposed support to accepted first articles, validated process capability, and delivered lots. Report first-pass yield, actual lead times, and repeat orders.
4. **Site economics.** Compare pilot demand with equipment utilization, power and cooling requirements, staffing, and the cost of in-house versus contracted processes. Underwrite facility expansion with buyer and tenant commitments.
5. **Policy milestones.** Track the Section 232 progress update due in late April 2027, any subsequent action defining product or country scope, committee activity on the robotics bills, and follow-on ARM Institute opportunities.
